Premium domain names behave more like brand equity than commodities. When the name captures an emerging category — such as **polyintelligence** — valuation is driven by narrative ownership, not just length or TLD.

## The five valuation pillars

1. **Brandability** — Can a company say the name once and be remembered?
2. **Category fit** — Does the string map cleanly to a rising market?
3. **Extension quality** — `.com` still commands the deepest liquidity for global brands.
4. **Comparable sales** — Recent AI, crypto, and fintech domain comps set a floor and ceiling.
5. **Strategic scarcity** — One exact-match narrative domain cannot be cloned.

## Applying the framework to AI domains

Buyers searching for phrases like “buy .com domains,” “premium domain names,” and “[domain].com for sale” are usually evaluating brand infrastructure — not a speculative flip alone. An exact, visionary name reduces go-to-market friction for research labs, multi-agent platforms, and funds.

For **aipolyintelligence.com**, the asset names the next chapter beyond narrow AI. That narrative density is rare: most AI domains are descriptive (“ai-tools,” “ml-platform”) rather than paradigm-defining.

## Practical next steps for buyers

- Confirm clean title and escrow path before negotiating.
- Model the brand cost of *not* owning the category name over a 5–10 year horizon.
- Compare against paid acquisition of equivalent brand awareness.
